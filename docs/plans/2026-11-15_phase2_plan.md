# Phase 2 plan — what erodes robustness, and what a sampler can do about it

Written 2026-11-15, 12:14–14:10 PST, by the planning agent (Slurm job 43272890, node n3432,
`cpu-g2`, 4 CPUs, no GPU), from `JOURNAL.md`, `results/`, the run directories under
`/gscratch/scrubbed/sgvtc/ordering-ar-runs/` as read between 12:20 and 13:40 (sweeps D–G were
still running) and CPU pilots of my own (section 6).

**[V]** = computed or re-checked by me (recipes in section 7); **[J]** = from the journal;
**[C]** = conjecture. Radii are in 1/255; robust@2 is PGD-10 accuracy at 2/255; "averaged
weights" are the EMA (decay 0.99); "matched clean accuracy" compares at equal clean accuracy by
interpolating along a run's evaluations; a run's "offset" is its robustness minus the mean curve
of all runs at the same clean accuracy.

## 1. Conclusions

1. **Vulnerability is a global loss of input-space margin, not the work of particular samples.
   [V]** From epoch 2 to 14 the margin of correct training points grows 2.8x and its
   input-gradient norm 8x; the median boundary distance falls from 3.7 to 1.2. The 6,195 samples
   correct at every checkpoint go from 0.80 to 0.49 robust@2 and from 0.59 to 0.06 robust@4 while
   their margins nearly triple. Samples learned after epoch 4 are non-robust on arrival (table A).
2. **It is structured by class and by schedule. [V]** At the averaged weights 94% of the
   robust@2 lost between epochs 4 and 14 is lost in the seven easier classes (0.47 to 0.29),
   while bird, cat and deer go from 0.30 to 0.68 clean accuracy without ever being robust
   (table B). Per unit of clean accuracy gained, robust@2 falls by 0.5–0.8 in epochs 4–8,
   0.7–1.1 in epochs 8–14 and 1.5–3.0 during the final anneal (four runs, table C).
3. **Operations on the weights move the model off the curve; nothing done to the data has. [V]**
   At matched clean accuracy, averaged weights mid-run are 0.02–0.06 robust@2 and about 0.2 AUC
   above annealed endpoints (z 2.3–3.0, three seeds a side, table D). Interpolating the epoch-4
   average with the final weights (25:75) gives 0.728 clean / 0.315 robust@2 where the averaged
   path has 0.262 (one run, CPU pilot, table I). The reference for a sampler is "uniform
   sampling + averaging + stop anywhere + interpolation"; no experiment has used it yet.
4. **One-step replay does not measure the drift, at raw or at averaged weights. [V]** At raw
   iterates a random step raises the linearised radius at seven of eight checkpoints while
   training loses it, and the rule that looks best in one step (keep high-radius samples, 8/8
   checkpoints) is among the worst in full training. At the averaged weights the effect of a
   random step on robust@2 changes sign between checkpoints (+, −, +, −, −), and no rule,
   scale-free ones included, improves robust accuracy at matched clean accuracy at more than two
   of five checkpoints (table F). Paired K-step branches with the average carried do measure it
   (conclusion 7).
5. **Per-sample scores are class competition. [V]** At the averaged weights a sample's
   first-order effect is stable between adjacent checkpoints (rank correlation 0.66–0.96; raw
   iterates flip sign), but what is stable is "class times gradient norm": class means correlate
   0.74–0.97 across checkpoints and half of all samples raise held-out clean loss. Loss-based
   and radius-based "robustness-only" scores rank the classes oppositely (cat +1.0 against −1.2
   sd). No sample is robustness-harming independently of how robustness is scored (table G).
6. **All rules tried sit on or below the curve, and they are class reweightings in disguise.
   [V]** The high-loss half of a pool is 18% cats and 4.5% cars; the low-loss half the reverse
   (table E). Difficulty has not been separated from class exposure.
7. **After about epoch 7 a run is on rails; which rails is decided early. [V]** Three uniform
   branches from one epoch-7 checkpoint end within 0.006 robust@2 and 0.010 AUC (sd); seed means
   differ by 0.012 and 0.040, reruns of a seed by 0.007 and 0.017. A run's mid-run offset
   predicts its late offset (r = 0.91, ten runs) and seeds separate during epochs 3–6 (table J).
   A one-epoch easy-only detour at epoch 10 (−12.7 points clean, 0.06 below the curve) is back
   on the curve one uniform epoch later (table H). **The order, not the initial weights, sets
   the level:** from the initial weights of the most robust seed, two other orders end at late
   offsets −0.028 and −0.014 robust@2 where the original order gives +0.010 (sweep G, first two
   runs), and two more are 0.02–0.04 below the original at epoch 7 (CPU pilot, table K).
   Class-balanced batches leave the curve where it is but cut the spread between seeds to about
   a third (three seeds a side: suggestive), so class composition may carry much of the order
   effect.
8. **Verdict. [C, from 3–7]** Under standard training of this model, selection after the early
   window moves the model along its curve. Order does matter, but early and so far only by
   luck: runs differ by ±0.02 robust@2 at matched clean accuracy because of it. The open
   question for a sampler is whether a good early order can be chosen rather than drawn; class
   structure is the second, weaker lever. By the bounds I can compute (spread between orders;
   table B) each is worth at most 0.03–0.06 robust@2, which weight-space operations already
   deliver without selection.
9. **Best honest deliverable.** (a) This characterisation, negative parts included; (b) the
   paired-branch harness that judges an ordering rule in minutes; (c) the fair frontier recipe:
   average, stop before the anneal, interpolate with an early checkpoint; (d) class-balanced
   batches for consistency, plus the sampler family of section 3 with kill criteria; (e) the
   same sampler on top of adversarial training as a separate track, the only place where
   "consistently adversarially robust" is attainable (section 3.6).

## 3. Method

### 3.1 What the data leave open

One-step scores and held-out loss gradients are ruled out as guides (conclusions 4–5; `pgRob`
collapsed [J]; radius-gradient selection stalls, table E). Reweighting by difficulty after the
early window moves along the curve (6–7). What does move a run is its early order (7), and what
has not been tested is class composition as such.

### 3.2 Part A: order selection by lookahead in the early window

Until the window closes, the trainer runs k candidate continuations of L steps from the current
state, each with a different order (uniform draws at first; class-composition schedules once #1
shows whether composition carries the effect), carrying the weight average. Each candidate is
scored on `probe` by the robustness of its averaged weights at matched clean accuracy: the
residual of its AUC from the line through the k candidates' (clean accuracy, AUC) points. The
best is kept and the procedure repeats; afterwards sampling is class-balanced. The final weights
are one ordinary SGD trajectory on clean training samples, and the chosen index sequence can be
replayed through a plain sampler. It needs snapshot and restore of model and optimiser around
`next_batch`, which the branch facility provides.

The weak point is visibility: a candidate's level must show by the end of the window. Over ten
runs the mid-run offset predicts the late one (r = 0.91), but the first sweep-G run was only
0.007 below at mid-run and 0.028 below at the end. If #3 finds the level is not visible early,
what remains is choosing among k complete runs on `probe`: honest, k times the cost, and no
more than seed selection.

### 3.3 Part B: class exposure x within-class band

* **Dial 1, class exposure.** Each batch has a prescribed class composition: balanced (default);
  hard classes at 0.5–0.75 of their share; or feedback, moving exposure away from the class whose
  accuracy currently costs the most robust accuracy on `probe`.
* **Dial 2, within-class band.** Candidates are drawn per class and scored by their own
  linearised radius in units of the selection radius, at the averaged weights; within each class
  the sampler drops the robustly fitted tail, the far-wrong tail, or both.

### 3.4 Hyperparameters that matter

Part A: k (4), L (351–702 steps), the window end (when uniform replicates stop diverging; about
epoch 6 here), the probe metric. Part B: keep fraction (0.75–0.9; 0.5 cost 13 points of clean
accuracy in one epoch), which tail is dropped, class floor, selection radius (so that 20–50% of
correct probe points are robust), start time.

### 3.5 Safeguards

* **Collapse.** No held-out loss gradients; scale-free scores only; class floor of half the
  uniform share.
* **Stalled learning.** Part A trains on unfiltered batches throughout. Part B keeps ≥ 0.75 of
  each pool and reverts to uniform for an epoch when probe clean accuracy of the averaged
  weights trails a stored reference by more than a point.
* **Merely trading accuracy for robustness.** Part A scores a residual at matched clean accuracy
  and never picks a candidate below the median clean accuracy. Every arm is judged against the
  envelope of #2, with per-class and worst-class accuracy.
* **Selecting noise.** Part A is dropped if probe and `val` offsets of candidates do not
  correlate.
* **Step length.** Part B is compared with the baseline at 0.5x, 1x and 2x its learning rate
  (journal pitfall 2).

### 3.6 Cost, odds, and adversarial training

Part A costs (k − 1) extra passes over the window: +90% compute for k = 4 and six epochs. Part B
costs one forward and one input-gradient pass over the pool, 2–2.5x wall-clock. Expected gain of
part A with k = 4: about one sd of the spread between orders, +0.012 to +0.02 robust@2 and
+0.04 AUC at matched clean accuracy, with a narrower spread between runs. Odds of beating the
fair frontier by ≥ 0.02 robust@2 over 10 seeds: part A one in three (it is a search over
orders, not yet an insight into them); part B one in five.

**On top of adversarial training: yes, as a second track.** Standard training ends at 0.000
robust accuracy at 8/255 and 0.013 at 4/255 whatever the sampler does [J]; the most robust
averaged model seen has 0.35 at 2/255 with 0.57 clean accuracy. An assessor asking for
adversarially robust models will not find them here. Under adversarial training the same
questions have real stakes (does robust accuracy decay after the lr drop, which classes pay),
the sampler seam is unchanged, and scores come almost free because the trainer already attacks
the batch. Whether "gradients of the chosen training samples" may be taken at perturbed inputs
is the owner's decision; I recommend allowing it as a clearly separated track, gated by #6.
Published sample-reweighting schemes for adversarial training have reported gains that weakened
under stronger attacks (from memory; check before citing), so strong-attack reporting is
mandatory there.

## 4. Evaluation protocol

1. **Curves, not points.** Each arm contributes the operating points of its averaged weights at
   every epoch of 5/10/20/40-epoch runs. Primary outcomes, fixed in advance: robust@2 and AUC
   over {0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8} at clean accuracy 0.70, 0.75, 0.79 (interpolated per seed), as
   differences from the baseline envelope with bootstrap intervals over seeds; also clean
   accuracy at matched robust accuracy.
2. **Baseline envelope.** Uniform sampling with the best of: epochs 5–40, lr x{0.5, 1, 2}, weight
   decay x{0, 1, 4}, averaging decay 0.99/0.999, no anneal, checkpoint interpolation; tuned on
   `val` with as many trials as the method. Each method arm is also reported with the same
   weight-space operations, to isolate the sampler's contribution.
3. **Seeds and power. [V]** At matched clean accuracy the sd of robust@2 between full runs is
   0.024 (annealed endpoints) and 0.014–0.027 (averaged weights): 3 seeds detect 0.055, 5 detect
   0.042, 10 detect 0.030 (80% power). Seed offsets are reproducible, so pair by seed when the
   sampler leaves the early order alone; screen with shared-checkpoint branches (replicate sd
   0.006); claim with 10 seeds.
4. **Attacks stronger than the selection signal.** Selection sees a linearised radius or PGD on
   `train`/`probe`. Reports use `arorder.evaluate` on all final models and AutoAttack on headline
   ones, on the untouched test split, at 1, 2, 4, 8. Masking checks: accuracy reaches zero as
   the radius grows; more steps and restarts change nothing; black-box and transfer attacks are
   no stronger than white-box.
5. **Splits and budgets.** `probe` selects, `val` tunes and interpolates, `test` is read once.
   Equal updates in the final trajectory, with total compute reported; in a second comparison
   the baseline spends the method's extra compute (for part A: best of k runs chosen by clean
   accuracy, and a k-times longer run).
6. **Generality.** ResNet-20 with GroupNorm and BatchNorm (eval-mode scoring differs under
   BatchNorm), then CIFAR-100 or SVHN, radii rescaled so that the baseline's peak robust
   accuracy is 20–50%.
7. **Reporting.** Per-class and worst-class accuracy (part B reallocates between classes); the
   spread between seeds as an outcome in its own right (the assessor's "consistently"); negative
   results in the same table as positive ones.
